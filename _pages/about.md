I'm **Arkajyoti (AJ) Bhattacharjee**, a Ph.D. candidate in Statistics (with a graduate minor in Computer Science) and a Presidential Fellow at [The Ohio State University](https://stat.osu.edu/), advised by Prof. [Arnab Auddy](https://arnab-auddy.github.io/).

**I am on the job market** for full-time positions starting after my Ph.D. (expected August 2027), as a research or applied scientist in machine learning and statistics, a data scientist or statistician, or a quantitative researcher in finance and risk modeling. My [CV](/files/CV.pdf) has the details, and I'd be glad to hear from you by [email](mailto:bhattacharjee.57@osu.edu).

My research develops statistical inference for unsupervised learning and generative models, with a focus on privacy, population comparison, and memorization. Much of my work centers on density modes, the local peaks of a probability density, which represent characteristic patterns in data, such as the distinct gene-activity profiles of different cancer types. I study how to estimate all of these modes while formally protecting the privacy of every individual record, and how to test whether two populations share the same mode locations even when the proportion of observations near each mode differs. Privacy concerns also arise in generative models: a model trained on patient scans can produce a synthetic image that reproduces a real patient's scan. I develop tests that distinguish this memorization from the natural similarity between independent individuals, along with theory that explains when memorization occurs. For each of these problems, I prove theoretical guarantees and evaluate the methods on real data.

I grew up in the city of sweets, Kolkata, India, and received my B.S. in Statistics from [Presidency University, Kolkata](https://www.presiuniv.ac.in/). I then went on to [IIT Kanpur](https://www.iitk.ac.in/) for my M.S. in Statistics, where working with Prof. [Dootika Vats](https://dvats.github.io/) on adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo gave me my first experience of research. During my master's, I also spent a summer putting statistics into practice. Through [Google Summer of Code](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/), I worked on improving R's nonlinear least-squares tools, which led to a paper in *The R Journal*. At [Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/), I built an AutoML tool in Python as a data science analyst intern. It was my research at IIT Kanpur, though, that led me to pursue a Ph.D., and in 2022 I came to Ohio State. In Summer 2026, I returned to industry as a Summer Associate at [JPMorgan Chase](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/), building machine-learning models for credit-risk decisions.

Outside of research, I spend most of my free time weightlifting and doing calisthenics.
